\section{Design and System Plans}
\label{sec:design}

Section~3.2 records the design routines behind the implemented
QueueFlow prototype. Section~\ref{sec:architecture} (\S2.8.3)
identifies the major layers and responsibilities; this section moves
one level closer to the code by stating each routine's inputs, outputs,
equations, parameter values, and pseudocode when the control flow
would otherwise be hard to follow. The discussion follows the
detection-to-ticket flow in Figure~\ref{fig:detection-ticket-pipeline}
(Figure~2.2): camera-frame filtering, queue-zone confirmation,
tracker repair, re-identification, queue-state updates, prediction,
ticket output, public display output, and the parameter summary.
Pseudocode blocks are placed after the equations and guard conditions
they use so that each algorithm stays with its own design step.

\subsection{Notation}
\label{subsec:notation}

The specifications below adopt the following notation. Queueing-theoretic
symbols ($\lambda$, $\mu$, $c$, $\rho$, $W$) retain the meanings introduced
in Section~\ref{sec:theoretical} (\S1.5). Queue length is denoted $Q$
throughout this section, consistent with the algorithmic literature;
$Q$ is identical to $L$ in Equation~\ref{eq:littles_law}. Two distinct smoothing
factors appear and must not be confused: $\alpha_{\mathrm{H}}$ and
$\beta_{\mathrm{H}}$ for Holt's double-exponential smoothing of queue
counts (\S\ref{subsec:prediction}), and $\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}$ for the
per-frame exponential moving average applied to bounding-box coordinates
(\S\ref{subsec:cv-layer}). Thresholds are denoted $\tau$ with a
descriptive subscript ($\tau_{\mathrm{tb}}$ for the appearance tiebreak,
$\tau_{\mathrm{twin}}$ for the twin guard, $\tau_{\mathrm{bl}}$ for the
done blacklist, $\tau_{\mathrm{iou}}$ for the duplicate-suppression IoU
gate). The intersection-over-union of two axis-aligned bounding boxes
$A$ and $B$ is

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{IoU}(A, B) \;=\; \frac{|A \cap B|}{|A \cup B|},
\label{eq:iou}
\end{equation}

\noindent used by the duplicate-suppression step (\S\ref{subsec:dedup}),
the track-identifier remapping step (\S\ref{subsec:remap}), and the
non-maximum suppression step internal to the tracker
(\S\ref{subsec:cv-layer}).

\subsection{Computer Vision Layer}
\label{subsec:cv-layer}

The Computer Vision Layer runs as a separate Python process
(\texttt{app/detector.py}) on the camera-connected machine. Each
captured frame is fed to a YOLOv8n detector~\cite{jocher2023}
configured for single-class person detection. Multi-object tracking is
performed by ByteTrack~\cite{zhang2022bytetrack}, the default tracker
when the Ultralytics interface is invoked with \texttt{persist=True};
ByteTrack assigns a persistent track identifier $\mathrm{tid}$ to each
detected person across frames. Raw YOLO output is then passed through
five quality gates before any detection is forwarded to the Business
Logic Layer.

\subsubsection{Detection quality gates}

For a bounding box with corners $(x_1, y_1, x_2, y_2)$ in a frame of
size $W_f \times H_f$, the five gates are defined as follows. Gate~1
requires sufficient area:

\begin{equation}
A \;=\; (x_2 - x_1)(y_2 - y_1) \;\geq\; A_{\min}.
\label{eq:gate1}
\end{equation}

\noindent Gate~2 caps the fraction of the frame a single box may
occupy:

\begin{equation}
f \;=\; \frac{A}{W_f \, H_f} \;\leq\; f_{\max}.
\end{equation}

\noindent Gate~3 enforces a portrait-orientation aspect ratio, which
removes most false positives caused by long horizontal objects:

\begin{equation}
r \;=\; \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} \;\geq\; r_{\min}.
\end{equation}

\noindent Gate~4 requires evidence of motion, computed as the number
of pixels whose grey-level difference between consecutive frames
exceeds a threshold:

\begin{equation}
M \;=\; \big|\{p : |g_t(p) - g_{t-1}(p)| > 25\}\big|.
\label{eq:motion}
\end{equation}

\noindent A detection passes Gate~4 if $M \geq M_{\min}$ or, as an
override for static high-confidence detections, if the detector
confidence satisfies $\text{conf} \geq c_{\mathrm{byp}}$. Gate~5 is
standard non-maximum suppression (NMS) applied internally by
ByteTrack using Equation~(\ref{eq:iou}). Only detections that pass
all five gates reach the queue tracker. Parameter values are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:cv-params}.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\footnotesize
\caption{Detection quality filtering}
\label{alg:detection-quality}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require current frame $F_t$, previous grey frame $G_{t-1}$, raw detections $D$
\Ensure filtered detections ready for queue-zone processing
\State $G_t \gets$ grey-scale version of $F_t$
\State $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ok}} \gets \emptyset$
\ForAll{$d \in D$ with box $(x_1,y_1,x_2,y_2)$ and confidence $\text{conf}$}
  \State $A \gets (x_2-x_1)(y_2-y_1)$; $f \gets A/(W_fH_f)$; $r \gets (y_2-y_1)/(x_2-x_1)$
  \If{$A < A_{\min}$ \textbf{or} $f > f_{\max}$ \textbf{or} $r < r_{\min}$}
    \State \textbf{continue}
  \EndIf
  \State compute motion energy $M$ from $G_t$ and $G_{t-1}$ using Equation~\ref{eq:motion}
  \If{$M < M_{\min}$ \textbf{and} $\text{conf} < c_{\mathrm{byp}}$}
    \State \textbf{continue}
  \EndIf
  \State append $d$ to $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ok}}$
\EndFor
\State apply ByteTrack/NMS to $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ok}}$
\State \Return filtered tracked detections
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Computer Vision Layer parameters.}
\label{tab:cv-params}
\begin{tabular}{lcl}
\hline
Symbol & Value & Description \\
\hline
$A_{\min}$       & $800\ \mathrm{px}^2$ & Minimum bounding-box area (Gate~1) \\
$f_{\max}$       & $0.85$               & Maximum frame coverage (Gate~2) \\
$r_{\min}$       & $0.60$               & Minimum portrait aspect ratio (Gate~3) \\
$M_{\min}$       & $8\ \mathrm{px}$     & Minimum motion energy (Gate~4) \\
$c_{\mathrm{byp}}$ & $0.70$             & Static-detection confidence bypass (Gate~4) \\
$\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}$ & $0.45$          & Bounding-box EMA smoothing factor \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsubsection{Bounding-box smoothing}

Raw YOLO bounding boxes jitter from frame to frame even when the
underlying person is moving smoothly. Each of the four box coordinates
is therefore smoothed with an exponential moving average:

\begin{equation}
\hat{b}_t \;=\; \alpha_{\mathrm{s}}\, b_t \;+\; (1 - \alpha_{\mathrm{s}})\, \hat{b}_{t-1},
\label{eq:ema-bbox}
\end{equation}

\noindent applied independently to $x_1$, $y_1$, $x_2$, and $y_2$.
The choice $\alpha_{\mathrm{s}} = 0.45$ is a practical midpoint for
walking-speed motion at $12$--$15$~fps: a higher $\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}$
reacts faster but appears jittery, a lower value is smoother but lags
behind. The smoothed boxes are the ones rendered into the annotated
snapshot that the staff dashboard displays.

\subsection{Queue Zone Membership and Candidate Confirmation}
\label{subsec:candidate}

A configurable rectangular region of interest---the queue zone---is
defined by four pixel coordinates $(z_{x_1}, z_{y_1}, z_{x_2}, z_{y_2})$
on the camera frame. A detected person is considered \emph{inside} the
zone when the centroid of their bounding box, $(c_x, c_y) = \big((x_1
+ x_2)/2,\ (y_1 + y_2)/2\big)$, falls within the rectangle:

\begin{equation}
\text{inside} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; z_{x_1} \leq c_x \leq z_{x_2}
\;\wedge\; z_{y_1} \leq c_y \leq z_{y_2}.
\label{eq:zone}
\end{equation}

\noindent The centroid is preferred over any single edge because the
centroid does not flicker into the zone when only an arm or shoulder
crosses the boundary.

A person who has just entered the queue zone is not assigned a queue
number immediately. Each new track identifier is buffered in a
candidate dictionary $C$ and must satisfy two conditions before a
number is issued: persistence and motion. Persistence requires that
the same $\mathrm{tid}$ remain inside the zone for at least
$N_{\mathrm{conf}}$ consecutive frames. Motion is assessed from the
standard deviations of the buffered centroid sequence
$\{(c_{x,i}, c_{y,i})\}_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{conf}}}$:

\begin{equation}
\sigma_x \;=\; \mathrm{std}(c_{x,1}, \ldots, c_{x,N_{\mathrm{conf}}}),
\qquad
\sigma_y \;=\; \mathrm{std}(c_{y,1}, \ldots, c_{y,N_{\mathrm{conf}}}),
\label{eq:sigma}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\text{motion ok} \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
\sigma_x + \sigma_y > \theta_{\mathrm{m}}
\;\vee\;
\overline{\text{conf}} \geq c_{\mathrm{byp}}.
\label{eq:motion-gate}
\end{equation}

\noindent Algorithm~\ref{alg:candidate} formalises the confirmation
procedure used after the zone and motion tests above.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\footnotesize
\caption{Candidate confirmation}
\label{alg:candidate}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require detection $d$ with track ID $\mathrm{tid}$ and confidence
$\text{conf}$, candidate buffer $C$, threshold $N_{\mathrm{conf}}$
\Ensure issue queue number, continue accumulating, or reject as static
\State $C[\mathrm{tid}].\text{count} \gets C[\mathrm{tid}].\text{count} + 1$
\State append $(c_x, c_y)$ to $C[\mathrm{tid}].\text{centers}$
\State append $\text{conf}$ to $C[\mathrm{tid}].\text{confs}$
\If{$C[\mathrm{tid}].\text{count} < N_{\mathrm{conf}}$}
  \State \Return continue accumulating
\EndIf
\State compute $\sigma_x, \sigma_y$ over $C[\mathrm{tid}].\text{centers}$
\State $\overline{\text{conf}} \gets \mathrm{mean}(C[\mathrm{tid}].\text{confs})$
\If{$\sigma_x + \sigma_y > \theta_{\mathrm{m}}$ \textbf{or} $\overline{\text{conf}} \geq c_{\mathrm{byp}}$}
  \State assign next queue number to $\mathrm{tid}$
\Else
  \State delete $C[\mathrm{tid}]$ \Comment{static object rejected}
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent At $N_{\mathrm{conf}} = 20$ frames and a typical frame rate
of $12$--$15$~fps, a person must remain in the zone for approximately
$1.3$--$1.7$~s before receiving a queue number. The motion threshold
$\theta_{\mathrm{m}} = 1.5\ \mathrm{px}$ rejects static objects (signs,
chairs, items left on the floor) that YOLO occasionally mis-classifies
as persons; the confidence bypass at $0.70$ admits a momentarily
stationary person whose detection is otherwise unambiguous.

\subsection{Track-Identifier Remapping}
\label{subsec:remap}

ByteTrack occasionally assigns a new $\mathrm{tid}$ to a person who
briefly left and re-entered the camera frame. Before any unfamiliar
identifier is forwarded to the queue tracker as a candidate,
\texttt{remap\_track\_ids} attempts to recover the original identifier
by comparing the new detection's bounding box to those of currently
active queue persons. Two signals are used: IoU (Equation~\ref{eq:iou})
and centroid Euclidean distance,

\begin{equation}
d(A, B) \;=\; \sqrt{(c_x^A - c_x^B)^2 + (c_y^A - c_y^B)^2}.
\label{eq:cdist}
\end{equation}

\noindent A remap score is computed with IoU prioritised over distance:

\begin{equation}
s \;=\;
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{IoU}(A, B) + 1.0 & \text{if } \mathrm{IoU}(A, B) \geq \tau_{\mathrm{iou}}^{\mathrm{rm}}, \\[2pt]
1 - d(A, B)/d_{\max}     & \text{else if } d(A, B) \leq d_{\max}, \\[2pt]
\text{(no match)}        & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:remap}
\end{equation}

\noindent The additive constant $1.0$ in the IoU branch guarantees that
any genuine overlap always outranks a distance-only match. The
candidate with the highest score above either threshold inherits the
known identifier. With $\tau_{\mathrm{iou}}^{\mathrm{rm}} = 0.10$ and
$d_{\max} = 180\ \mathrm{px}$, remapping rescues brief tracker hiccups
without false matches across the zone.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\footnotesize
\caption{Track-identifier remapping}
\label{alg:remap}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require incoming detection $d$ with new track ID $\mathrm{tid}_{new}$, active queue $\mathcal{Q}$
\Ensure original track ID if recoverable, otherwise $\mathrm{tid}_{new}$
\State $\mathrm{bestID} \gets \mathrm{tid}_{new}$; $\mathrm{bestScore} \gets -\infty$
\ForAll{$p \in \mathcal{Q}$ with active or recently visible status}
  \State $u \gets \mathrm{IoU}(d.\mathrm{bbox}, p.\mathrm{bbox})$
  \State $\delta \gets d(d.\mathrm{bbox}, p.\mathrm{bbox})$
  \If{$u \geq \tau_{\mathrm{iou}}^{\mathrm{rm}}$}
    \State $s \gets u + 1.0$
  \ElsIf{$\delta \leq d_{\max}$}
    \State $s \gets 1 - \delta/d_{\max}$
  \Else
    \State \textbf{continue}
  \EndIf
  \If{$s > \mathrm{bestScore}$}
    \State $\mathrm{bestScore} \gets s$; $\mathrm{bestID} \gets p.\mathrm{tid}$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $\mathrm{bestID}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Duplicate Suppression}
\label{subsec:dedup}

A second class of tracker artefact---distinct from a re-entered
person---is the \emph{ghost track}: two different $\mathrm{tid}$ values
assigned to the same physical person within the same frame. Ghost
tracks must be merged in the active queue, retaining the lower queue
number. The merge condition combines bounding-box overlap and centroid
proximity:

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{dup}(p_i, p_j) \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
\mathrm{IoU}(p_i, p_j) > \tau_{\mathrm{iou}}^{\mathrm{dd}}
\;\vee\;
d(p_i, p_j) < f_{\mathrm{dd}} \cdot \overline{D}(p_i, p_j),
\label{eq:dedup}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $\overline{D}(p_i, p_j)$ is the mean diagonal of the
two bounding boxes and $f_{\mathrm{dd}}$ is the centre-fraction
threshold. Two safeguards prevent over-aggressive merging.
First, an immunity counter is set to $I_0 = 60$~frames whenever a
queue entry is restored via re-identification (\S\ref{subsec:reid});
while the counter is positive, the entry is exempt from
deduplication. Second, even when Equation~(\ref{eq:dedup}) is
satisfied, the merge is rejected if the appearance similarity
(Equation~\ref{eq:psim}) between the two entries falls below
$\tau_{\mathrm{bl}} = 0.55$, on the grounds that two visually distinct
persons should not be collapsed regardless of their geometric
proximity. Parameter values are listed in Table~\ref{tab:dedup-params}.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Duplicate-suppression parameters.}
\label{tab:dedup-params}
\begin{tabular}{lcl}
\hline
Symbol & Value & Description \\
\hline
$\tau_{\mathrm{iou}}^{\mathrm{dd}}$ & $0.40$ & IoU threshold for ghost-track merge \\
$f_{\mathrm{dd}}$                   & $0.15$ & Centre-distance fraction of mean diagonal \\
$I_0$                               & $60$   & Re-entry immunity (frames) \\
$\tau_{\mathrm{bl}}$                & $0.55$ & Appearance guard against false merge \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:dedup} formalises the per-frame deduplication pass.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\footnotesize
\caption{Per-frame duplicate suppression}
\label{alg:dedup}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require active queue $\mathcal{Q}$, immunity table $I$
\ForAll{ordered pairs $(p_i, p_j) \in \mathcal{Q} \times \mathcal{Q}$ with $i < j$}
  \If{$I[p_i] > 0$ \textbf{or} $I[p_j] > 0$}
    \State \textbf{continue} \Comment{immunity active}
  \EndIf
  \If{appearance signatures exist \textbf{and} $\mathrm{sim}(p_i, p_j) < \tau_{\mathrm{bl}}$}
    \State \textbf{continue} \Comment{visually distinct}
  \EndIf
  \If{$\mathrm{dup}(p_i, p_j)$ holds (Equation~\ref{eq:dedup})}
    \State drop the higher queue number, keep the lower
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State decrement every positive entry of $I$ by one
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Appearance-Based Re-identification}
\label{subsec:reid}

Re-identification recovers the original queue number when a person
leaves the camera frame for longer than the tracker can bridge, then
returns. The procedure has four parts: an appearance signature
(\S\ref{subsubsec:signature}), a comparison metric
(\S\ref{subsubsec:psim}), a hybrid decision rule
(\S\ref{subsubsec:hybrid}), and two guards against false matches
(\S\ref{subsubsec:guards}).

\subsubsection{Appearance signature}
\label{subsubsec:signature}

For each queued person, the bounding-box crop is divided horizontally
at its midpoint and a $16 \times 16$ joint Hue--Saturation histogram is
computed for each half in the HSV colour space:

\begin{equation}
H(b_h, b_s) \;=\; \big|\{p : \text{hue}(p) \in B_h \wedge \text{sat}(p) \in B_s\}\big|,
\label{eq:hist-bin}
\end{equation}

\noindent with $b_h \in \{0, \ldots, 15\}$ partitioning the OpenCV hue
range $[0, 180)$ and $b_s \in \{0, \ldots, 15\}$ partitioning the
saturation range $[0, 256)$. Each half-histogram is normalised so its
bin values sum to one. The full appearance signature
$\mathrm{sig} \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$ is the concatenation of the upper
and lower half-histograms. HSV is preferred over RGB because
separating chroma from luminance makes the signature more robust to
indoor--outdoor lighting variation. The signature is updated every
$30$~s of wait time using an exponential moving average:

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{sig}_t \;=\; 0.6\, \mathrm{sig}_{t-1} + 0.4\, \mathrm{sig}_{\text{new}}.
\label{eq:sig-ema}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Comparison metric}
\label{subsubsec:psim}

Two signatures are compared using the Pearson histogram correlation
applied to each body half:

\begin{equation}
r(H_1, H_2) \;=\;
\frac{\sum_i (H_{1,i} - \bar{H}_1)(H_{2,i} - \bar{H}_2)}
     {\sqrt{\sum_i (H_{1,i} - \bar{H}_1)^2} \, \sqrt{\sum_i (H_{2,i} - \bar{H}_2)^2}},
\label{eq:pearson}
\end{equation}

\noindent and combined with a fixed body-half weighting that favours
the upper body, which carries more person-distinctive colour
information (shirts, jackets) than the lower body:

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{sim}(\mathrm{sig}_1, \mathrm{sig}_2)
\;=\;
0.6\, r(H_1^{\mathrm{upper}}, H_2^{\mathrm{upper}}) +
0.4\, r(H_1^{\mathrm{lower}}, H_2^{\mathrm{lower}}).
\label{eq:psim}
\end{equation}

\noindent The metric ranges over $[-1, 1]$. Three thresholds are
applied across the system: $\tau_{\mathrm{tb}} = 0.30$ for the
re-identification tiebreak (\S\ref{subsubsec:hybrid}),
$\tau_{\mathrm{bl}} = 0.55$ for the done-blacklist match
(\S\ref{subsubsec:guards}), and $\tau_{\mathrm{twin}} = 0.85$ for the
twin/lookalike guard (\S\ref{subsubsec:guards}).

\subsubsection{Hybrid decision rule}
\label{subsubsec:hybrid}

When a new track identifier appears that did not match any active
queue person under remapping (\S\ref{subsec:remap}), the re-identifier
considers all missing-person records eligible within the
$T_{\mathrm{rec}} = 120\ \mathrm{s}$ recency window. The decision
rule has two branches.

In the single-eligible-missing-person branch, the appearance
comparison is applied first: if $\mathrm{sim}(\mathrm{sig}, \mathrm{sig}_{\text{new}})
\geq \tau_{\mathrm{tb}}$, the missing queue number is restored. If
appearance is inconclusive, a spatial fallback applies: with
normalised spatial score

\begin{equation}
s_{\text{sp}}(b_1, b_2) \;=\; \frac{d(b_1, b_2)}{\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\mathrm{diag}(b_1) + \mathrm{diag}(b_2)\big)},
\label{eq:spatial}
\end{equation}

\noindent the queue number is restored if $s_{\text{sp}} <
s_{\mathrm{fb}}^{\mathrm{app}}$, with $s_{\mathrm{fb}}^{\mathrm{app}}
= 0.20$ (a centroid offset of roughly one-fifth of an average
bounding-box diagonal). If neither condition holds, a new queue
number is issued.

In the multi-eligible-missing-person branch, the spatial fallback is
deliberately omitted: a person who returns to a different queue
position would otherwise be matched to the entry that happens to sit
nearest in pixel space, which is precisely the wrong record. Instead,
the appearance scores of all eligible missing persons are ranked, and
restoration is committed only when the best score $s^{(1)}$ exceeds
$\tau_{\mathrm{tb}}$ \emph{and} exceeds the second-best score
$s^{(2)}$ by at least $\Delta_{\mathrm{mm}} = 0.10$:

\begin{equation}
\text{restore} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; s^{(1)} \geq \tau_{\mathrm{tb}} \;\wedge\; s^{(1)} - s^{(2)} \geq \Delta_{\mathrm{mm}}.
\label{eq:multi-reid}
\end{equation}

\noindent Otherwise the system declines to guess and issues a new
number. The branch logic above is applied only after the guard checks
below have not blocked the detection.

\subsubsection{Twin guard and done blacklist}
\label{subsubsec:guards}

Two guards run before any re-identification commit. The twin guard
fires when the new detection is nearly identical in appearance to a
person already visible in the active queue:

\begin{equation}
\exists\, p \in \mathcal{Q} \;:\; \mathrm{sim}(p.\mathrm{sig}, \mathrm{sig}_{\text{new}}) \geq \tau_{\mathrm{twin}}.
\label{eq:twin}
\end{equation}

\noindent When the condition holds, restoration is blocked and a fresh
queue number is issued; the rationale is that two persons with
near-identical clothing should not be merged into a single queue
entry. The done blacklist fires when the new detection's appearance
matches that of a person already served in the same session:

\begin{equation}
\exists\, b \in \mathcal{B} \;:\; \mathrm{sim}(b, \mathrm{sig}_{\text{new}}) \geq \tau_{\mathrm{bl}},
\label{eq:blacklist}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $\mathcal{B}$ is the set of appearance signatures of
served persons retained for the duration of the session. When the
condition holds, no new queue entry is created. The thresholds
$\tau_{\mathrm{twin}} = 0.85$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{bl}} = 0.55$
reflect the asymmetric tolerance these guards require: the twin guard
must be strict (only near-identical appearances trigger it) so that
ordinary returning persons are not mis-classified as twins, whereas
the done blacklist must be more permissive so that a recently served
person whose appearance has shifted slightly (due to lighting or
posture) is still recognised.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:reid} gives the complete re-identification order:
guard checks first, single-missing or multi-missing recovery second.
The calling queue service treats \textsc{Blocked} as no ticket and
\textsc{None} as a new entrant that may receive a fresh queue number.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\footnotesize
\caption{Hybrid re-identification (appearance-first with spatial fallback)}
\label{alg:reid}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require new detection signature $\mathrm{sig}_{\text{new}}$, box $b_{\text{new}}$, active queue $\mathcal{Q}$, served blacklist $\mathcal{B}$, eligible missing set $\mathcal{M}$
\Ensure restored queue person, \textsc{None}, or \textsc{Blocked}
\If{$\exists\, p \in \mathcal{Q}: \mathrm{sim}(p.\mathrm{sig}, \mathrm{sig}_{\text{new}}) \geq \tau_{\mathrm{twin}}$}
  \State \Return \textsc{None} \Comment{lookalike guard; issue a fresh number}
\EndIf
\If{$\exists\, b \in \mathcal{B}: \mathrm{sim}(b, \mathrm{sig}_{\text{new}}) \geq \tau_{\mathrm{bl}}$}
  \State \Return \textsc{Blocked} \Comment{already served in this session}
\EndIf
\If{$|\mathcal{M}| = 1$} \Comment{single-missing branch}
  \State $p \gets$ the unique element of $\mathcal{M}$
  \State $s \gets \mathrm{sim}(p.\mathrm{sig}, \mathrm{sig}_{\text{new}})$
  \If{$s \geq \tau_{\mathrm{tb}}$} \Return $p$ \Comment{appearance match}
  \EndIf
  \If{$s_{\text{sp}}(p.\text{bbox}, b_{\text{new}}) < s_{\mathrm{fb}}^{\mathrm{app}}$} \Return $p$ \Comment{spatial fallback}
  \EndIf
  \State \Return \textsc{None}
\Else \Comment{multi-missing branch}
  \State rank $\mathcal{M}$ by $\mathrm{sim}(\cdot, \mathrm{sig}_{\text{new}})$
  \State let $s^{(1)}, s^{(2)}$ be the top scores; let $p^{(1)}$ be the best match
  \If{$s^{(1)} \geq \tau_{\mathrm{tb}}$ \textbf{and} $s^{(1)} - s^{(2)} \geq \Delta_{\mathrm{mm}}$}
    \State \Return $p^{(1)}$
  \Else
    \State \Return \textsc{None} \Comment{ambiguous}
  \EndIf
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{No-Show Detection}
\label{subsec:noshow}

When the person at position~$1$ in the active queue becomes missing,
a countdown is started. If the person does not return before the
no-show window $T_{\mathrm{ns}} = 300\ \mathrm{s}$ elapses, the entry
is automatically marked as a no-show and bumped from the queue. Let
$\tau_{\mathrm{start}}(q)$ denote the moment the countdown for queue
entry $q$ begins. The elapsed time is

\begin{equation}
e(q) \;=\; t_{\text{now}} - \tau_{\mathrm{start}}(q),
\label{eq:noshow-elapsed}
\end{equation}

\noindent and the no-show condition is

\begin{equation}
\text{no\_show}(q) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; e(q) \geq T_{\mathrm{ns}}
\;\wedge\;
\text{position}(q) = 1
\;\wedge\;
\text{status}(q) = \texttt{missing}.
\label{eq:noshow}
\end{equation}

\noindent The countdown resets to zero if the person returns before
expiry. The dashboard displays the remaining time
$\max(0,\, T_{\mathrm{ns}} - e(q))$ and tags the alert as
\texttt{critical} when the remaining time falls below $15\ \mathrm{s}$
and \texttt{warning} otherwise. Only the position-$1$ entry is
monitored because only that entry is being called; persons further
back in the line may legitimately be waiting elsewhere within the
service area.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\footnotesize
\caption{No-show countdown and bumping}
\label{alg:noshow}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require active queue $\mathcal{Q}$, countdown table $S$, current time $t_{\mathrm{now}}$
\ForAll{$q \in \mathcal{Q}$}
  \If{$q.\mathrm{position}=1$ \textbf{and} $q.\mathrm{status}=\texttt{missing}$}
    \If{$q \notin S$}
      \State $S[q] \gets t_{\mathrm{now}}$
    \ElsIf{$t_{\mathrm{now}} - S[q] \geq T_{\mathrm{ns}}$}
      \State mark $q$ as \texttt{no\_show}
      \State move $q$ from active queue to completed/no-show records
      \State register $q$'s appearance in the done blacklist
      \State delete $S[q]$
    \EndIf
  \Else
    \State delete $S[q]$ if present
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Counter Assignment}
\label{subsec:counters}

After every frame, the active queue $\mathcal{Q}$ is sorted by queue
number in ascending order. Each person receives an integer
position-in-line and, if a counter is available, is assigned to one.
Counter assignment is \emph{sticky}: once a counter is allocated to a
queue entry, the allocation persists until that entry is marked done
or no-show, regardless of changes to the line behind it. Let
$\text{rank}(p)$ denote the $1$-based position of $p.\text{queue\_number}$
in the sorted active queue and let $c$ be the number of counters
configured for the service office. The assignment rule is

\begin{equation}
\text{counter}(p) \;=\;
\begin{cases}
\text{rank}(p) & \text{if } \text{rank}(p) \leq c \text{ and } p \text{ unassigned}, \\
\text{previous allocation} & \text{if } p \text{ already assigned}, \\
\text{(none)} & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:counter}
\end{equation}

\noindent The sticky rule is essential for user experience: without
it, every time the position-$1$ person is served, the remaining queue
would visibly shift one counter to the left, confusing both the
served-next student and the staff member at the affected counter.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:counter} formalises the per-frame procedure.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\footnotesize
\caption{Sticky counter assignment}
\label{alg:counter}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require active queue $\mathcal{Q}$, number of counters $c$
\State sort $\mathcal{Q}$ by queue number ascending; call this $L$
\For{$i = 1$ to $|L|$}
  \State $L[i].\text{position} \gets i$
\EndFor
\State $\mathrm{occupied} \gets \{p.\text{counter} : p \in L \text{ with } p.\text{counter} \neq \text{none}\}$
\State $\mathrm{free} \gets \{1, \ldots, c\} \setminus \mathrm{occupied}$, sorted ascending
\ForAll{$p \in L$ with $p.\text{counter} = \text{none}$}
  \If{$\mathrm{free}$ non-empty}
    \State $p.\text{counter} \gets$ pop smallest element of $\mathrm{free}$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Wait-Time Prediction}
\label{subsec:prediction}

The Prediction Service consumes a rolling buffer of up to sixty
crowd-count samples and updates the wait-time estimates every frame.
It first computes the current wait estimate with an M/M/c approximation,
then estimates the arrival rate, computes a local trend slope, and uses
those values for the short-, medium-, and long-horizon forecasts.
Section~\ref{subsec:service-time} (\S\ref{subsec:service-time})
describes the dynamic service-time measurement that supplies $\mu$ to
the M/M/c baseline.

\subsubsection{M/M/c baseline (current wait)}
\label{subsubsec:mmc-baseline}

With arrival rate $\lambda$, service rate $\mu$, and $c$ active
counters, the system utilisation $\rho = \lambda/(c\mu)$ is given by
Equation~\ref{eq:utilization}. When the system is stable ($\rho < 1$), the
current wait estimate combines Little's Law (Equation~\ref{eq:littles_law}) with the
expected service duration:

\begin{equation}
W_{\text{current}} \;=\;
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{Q}{\max(\lambda,\, 0.1)} + \dfrac{1}{\mu} & \text{if } \rho < 1, \\[10pt]
\dfrac{Q}{\mu \, c} & \text{if } \rho \geq 1.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:wcurrent}
\end{equation}

\noindent The saturated-system branch is a deterministic conservative
estimate used when arrivals exceed capacity, in which case the
classical M/M/c steady-state formulas no longer apply.

\subsubsection{Arrival rate}
\label{subsubsec:arrival-rate}

The arrival rate over the recent history is estimated from the
difference of queue counts $n$ samples apart, divided by the elapsed
time between those samples in minutes:

\begin{equation}
\lambda \;=\; \frac{Q_t - Q_{t-n}}{\Delta t}, \qquad n = \min(30,\, |\text{history}|).
\label{eq:arrival}
\end{equation}

\noindent Negative differences (departures exceeding arrivals) are
clamped to zero, and the value is recomputed every frame.

\subsubsection{Trend slope}
\label{subsubsec:trend-slope}

The short-horizon forecast requires a local rate of change in queue
count. A simple ordinary-least-squares regression is fit to the most
recent $W_{\mathrm{tr}} = 20$ samples after de-meaning the time
indices, yielding a no-intercept slope

\begin{equation}
\beta \;=\; \frac{\sum_i x_i \, y_i}{\sum_i x_i^2},
\qquad
x_i \;=\; i - \bar{x},
\label{eq:slope}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $y_i$ is the queue count at sample $i$. The slope is
expressed in persons per frame and is converted to persons per second
in the next sub-model via an estimated frame rate.

\subsubsection{Short-term forecast ($\approx 5\ \mathrm{min}$)}
\label{subsubsec:short-forecast}

The projected queue count $T_{\mathrm{sh}} = 600\ \mathrm{s}$ ahead is
obtained by linear extrapolation along the OLS slope:

\begin{equation}
\hat{Q}_{\text{short}} \;=\; \max\!\big(0,\, Q + \beta \cdot \mathrm{fps} \cdot T_{\mathrm{sh}}\big).
\label{eq:qshort}
\end{equation}

\noindent The projected wait time is then computed from
$\hat{Q}_{\text{short}}$ using the same M/M/c-style conversion as
Equation~(\ref{eq:wcurrent}). Linear extrapolation is appropriate at
this horizon because queue level rarely changes regime within
five minutes during normal service operation.

\subsubsection{Medium-term forecast ($\approx 15\ \mathrm{min}$)}
\label{subsubsec:holt-forecast}

For the medium horizon, Holt's double-exponential
smoothing~\cite{holt2004forecasting} is applied to the crowd-count buffer. The
level $L_t$ and the trend $T_t$ are updated jointly each frame:

\begin{align}
L_t &\;=\; \alpha_{\mathrm{H}} \, y_t \;+\; (1 - \alpha_{\mathrm{H}})\,(L_{t-1} + T_{t-1}), \label{eq:holt-level} \\
T_t &\;=\; \beta_{\mathrm{H}} \,(L_t - L_{t-1}) \;+\; (1 - \beta_{\mathrm{H}})\, T_{t-1}. \label{eq:holt-trend}
\end{align}

\noindent The forecast $h$ steps ahead is then damped by a factor
$\varphi < 1$ raised to the projection step, preventing the trend
component from over-projecting during transient spikes:

\begin{equation}
\hat{Q}_{t+h} \;=\; L_t \;+\; T_t \sum_{i=1}^{h} \varphi^i.
\label{eq:holt-forecast}
\end{equation}

\noindent The deployed values are $\alpha_{\mathrm{H}} = 0.4$,
$\beta_{\mathrm{H}} = 0.2$, $\varphi = 0.85$, and $h = 20$, yielding
$\sum_{i=1}^{20} 0.85^i \approx 5.67$. The projected wait time is
again obtained by applying the M/M/c-style conversion of
Equation~(\ref{eq:wcurrent}) to $\hat{Q}_{t+h}$.

\subsubsection{Long-term forecast ($\approx 30\ \mathrm{min}$)}
\label{subsubsec:long-forecast}

For the long horizon, linear or trend-only methods are
unreliable. QueueFlow instead blends a growth-ratio projection with
mean reversion to a recent anchor. The crowd-count history is split
into thirds, with $Q_{\mathrm{early}}$ and $Q_{\mathrm{recent}}$
denoting the means of the earliest and latest thirds respectively.
The growth ratio is

\begin{equation}
g \;=\; \frac{Q_{\mathrm{recent}}}{\max(Q_{\mathrm{early}},\, 1)},
\label{eq:growth}
\end{equation}

\noindent and the projected queue at the long horizon is

\begin{equation}
\hat{Q}_{\text{long}} \;=\; \max\!\Big(0,\; Q_{\mathrm{recent}} \cdot \big(0.7 \, g + 0.3\big)\Big),
\label{eq:qlong}
\end{equation}

\noindent placing $70\%$ of the weight on the observed growth trend
and $30\%$ on mean reversion to the recent level. The projected wait
time is then derived as in Equation~(\ref{eq:wcurrent}) and capped at
sixty minutes; long-horizon forecasts are inherently uncertain and the
cap prevents a single high-growth window from producing a meaningless
multi-hour estimate on the dashboard.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\footnotesize
\caption{Multi-horizon wait-time prediction}
\label{alg:prediction}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require count history $H$, current queue length $Q$, active counters $c$, service rate $\mu$
\Ensure current, short-, medium-, and long-horizon wait estimates
\State append current $Q$ and timestamp to $H$
\State compute $\lambda$ from recent count differences using Equation~\ref{eq:arrival}
\State $\rho \gets \lambda/(c\mu)$
\State $W_{\mathrm{current}} \gets$ Equation~\ref{eq:wcurrent}
\State compute OLS slope $\beta$ on the latest $W_{\mathrm{tr}}$ samples using Equation~\ref{eq:slope}
\State $\hat{Q}_{\mathrm{short}} \gets \max(0, Q+\beta\cdot \mathrm{fps}\cdot T_{\mathrm{sh}})$
\State $W_{\mathrm{short}} \gets$ convert $\hat{Q}_{\mathrm{short}}$ to wait time using Equation~\ref{eq:wcurrent}
\State $(L_t,T_t) \gets$ Holt update over $H$ using Equations~\ref{eq:holt-level}--\ref{eq:holt-trend}
\State $\hat{Q}_{t+h} \gets L_t + T_t \sum_{i=1}^{h}\varphi^i$
\State $W_{\mathrm{medium}} \gets$ convert $\hat{Q}_{t+h}$ to wait time using Equation~\ref{eq:wcurrent}
\State split $H$ into thirds and compute $Q_{\mathrm{early}}$ and $Q_{\mathrm{recent}}$
\State $g \gets Q_{\mathrm{recent}}/\max(Q_{\mathrm{early}},1)$
\State $\hat{Q}_{\mathrm{long}} \gets \max(0,Q_{\mathrm{recent}}(0.7g+0.3))$
\State $W_{\mathrm{long}} \gets \min(W_{\max}^{\mathrm{long}},$ converted wait from $\hat{Q}_{\mathrm{long}})$
\State \Return $(W_{\mathrm{current}}, W_{\mathrm{short}}, W_{\mathrm{medium}}, W_{\mathrm{long}}, \rho, \lambda)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Hybrid AI wait-time predictor}
\label{subsubsec:hybrid-ai-prediction}

The deployed AI model does not replace the analytical routines above.
Instead, QueueFlow treats their outputs as interpretable engineered
features for a supervised Gradient Boosting regressor. For each
training or live prediction row, the system computes queue-state
features, recent-demand features, academic-period indicators, and the
analytical prediction features
$W_{\mathrm{current}}$, $W_{\mathrm{short}}$, $W_{\mathrm{medium}}$,
and $W_{\mathrm{long}}$ from Algorithm~\ref{alg:prediction}. These
features are passed to a \texttt{HistGradientBoostingRegressor}, which
learns nonlinear corrections from historical queue behavior.

During live operation, the trained model is not retrained. The backend
calibrates the AI output with runtime evidence: current service speed,
active counters, yesterday's same-hour average wait, and recent
prediction error. This hybrid design keeps the prediction grounded in
the queueing and forecasting framework while allowing the model to
learn from historical data.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\footnotesize
\caption{Hybrid AI wait-time prediction}
\label{alg:hybrid-ai-prediction}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require current queue state, academic calendar, count history $H$, active counters $c$, trained Gradient Boosting model $G$
\Ensure calibrated student-facing wait-time estimate
\State compute queue-state features: queue number, people ahead, and daily queue size
\State compute recent-demand features: arrivals in the last 5 and 15 minutes
\State compute academic-period indicators from the academic calendar
\State compute analytical prediction features using Algorithm~\ref{alg:prediction}
\State concatenate all features into vector $x$
\State $W_{\mathrm{AI}} \gets G(x)$
\State compute recent prediction-error correction $e_{\mathrm{recent}}$
\State $W_{\mathrm{AI,corr}} \gets \max(0, W_{\mathrm{AI}} + e_{\mathrm{recent}})$
\State compute runtime baseline $W_{\mathrm{base}}$ from current service speed, active counters, and yesterday same-hour wait
\State $\gamma \gets \min(\mathrm{completed\_today}/10, 1)$ \Comment{confidence in current-day service pattern}
\State blend $W_{\mathrm{base}}$ toward current-day service behavior using $\gamma$
\State $W_{\mathrm{final}} \gets 0.6 W_{\mathrm{AI,corr}} + 0.4 W_{\mathrm{base}}$
\State \Return bounded $W_{\mathrm{final}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Dynamic Service-Time Measurement}
\label{subsec:service-time}

The service rate $\mu = 1 / \overline{\text{svc}}$ used in
Equation~(\ref{eq:wcurrent}) is not a static parameter. A background
thread re-measures the average service time every
$T_{\mathrm{svc}} = 300\ \mathrm{s}$ from the MySQL transaction log,
ensuring that the prediction component adapts to the actual pace of
service rather than to a single pre-deployment calibration.

For a sequence of completed-transaction timestamps $t_1, t_2, \ldots,
t_N$, inter-departure gaps in minutes are

\begin{equation}
g_i \;=\; \frac{t_i - t_{i-1}}{60}, \qquad i = 2, \ldots, N,
\label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}

\noindent and gaps outside the range $[0.1,\, 15]\ \mathrm{min}$ are
discarded as either database-write noise (very short) or cashier idle
periods (very long). The mean of the retained gaps is multiplied by
$c$ to correct for the fact that, in a parallel-server system,
the inter-departure interval observed at the aggregate output is
approximately $\overline{\text{svc}} / c$:

\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathrm{svc}} \;=\; \bar{g} \cdot c.
\label{eq:svc-measured}
\end{equation}

\noindent The new estimate is then blended with the previously stored
value so that a single unusually fast or slow batch of transactions
does not jolt the prediction:

\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathrm{svc}}_{\text{new}} \;=\; 0.7\,\widehat{\mathrm{svc}} + 0.3\,\widehat{\mathrm{svc}}_{\text{prev}}.
\label{eq:svc-blend}
\end{equation}

\noindent The pre-deployment calibration described in
Section~\ref{sec:datagathering} (\S2.4, Phase~1) supplies the initial
seed value for $\widehat{\mathrm{svc}}_{\text{prev}}$ before any
transactions have been logged.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\footnotesize
\caption{Dynamic service-time measurement}
\label{alg:service-time}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require served transaction timestamps $t_1,\ldots,t_N$, active counters $c$, previous service time $\widehat{\mathrm{svc}}_{\mathrm{prev}}$
\Ensure updated service time $\widehat{\mathrm{svc}}_{\mathrm{new}}$ and service rate $\mu$
\State $G \gets \emptyset$
\For{$i=2$ to $N$}
  \State $g_i \gets (t_i-t_{i-1})/60$
  \If{$0.1 \leq g_i \leq 15$}
    \State append $g_i$ to $G$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\If{$G$ has too few samples}
  \State \Return previous $\widehat{\mathrm{svc}}_{\mathrm{prev}}$ and $\mu=1/\widehat{\mathrm{svc}}_{\mathrm{prev}}$
\EndIf
\State $\widehat{\mathrm{svc}} \gets \mathrm{mean}(G)\cdot c$
\State $\widehat{\mathrm{svc}}_{\mathrm{new}} \gets 0.7\widehat{\mathrm{svc}}+0.3\widehat{\mathrm{svc}}_{\mathrm{prev}}$
\State $\mu \gets 1/\widehat{\mathrm{svc}}_{\mathrm{new}}$
\State \Return $(\widehat{\mathrm{svc}}_{\mathrm{new}}, \mu)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Ticket Generation}
\label{subsec:ticket}

When a queue number is confirmed, the Ticket Service generates a
printer-agnostic ticket payload comprising four fields: the queue
number, a short alphanumeric code for manual entry, a QR-encoded
status-page URL, and a JSON Web Token (JWT) that authenticates
subsequent status requests from the mobile client. During evaluation,
the payload is rendered as a PDF saved to a local \texttt{Tickets/}
directory. In office operation, the same queue record is sent to the
thermal printer and issued as a paper ticket. The PDF or printed ticket
is automatically deleted or voided when the entry is marked done or
no-show.

The JWT is constructed with the HS256 algorithm. The header and
payload are URL-safe base64-encoded JSON objects, concatenated by a
period, and signed with a server-side secret using HMAC-SHA256:

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{token} \;=\; h \cdot \texttt{.} \cdot p \cdot \texttt{.} \cdot \mathrm{base64url}\big(\mathrm{HMAC\text{-}SHA256}(h \cdot \texttt{.} \cdot p,\ K)\big),
\label{eq:jwt}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $h = \mathrm{base64url}(\{\texttt{alg}: \texttt{"HS256"},\, \texttt{typ}: \texttt{"JWT"}\})$,
$p = \mathrm{base64url}(\{\texttt{sub}, \texttt{iat}, \texttt{exp}, \texttt{jti}\})$, and
$K$ is the secret. The payload includes the queue number
(\texttt{sub}), the issue time (\texttt{iat}), an expiry four hours
later (\texttt{exp}), and a $32$-character hexadecimal nonce
(\texttt{jti}) that makes every issued token unique and prevents
replay of an intercepted token.

The short code is drawn uniformly at random from the $32$-character
alphabet $\{A,\ldots,Z\} \cup \{2,\ldots,9\} \setminus \{O, I\}$,
excluding the visually ambiguous characters $0$, $O$, $1$, and $I$ to
eliminate manual-entry errors. Eight characters are drawn and
displayed as \texttt{XXXX-XXXX}, providing entropy of
$\log_2(32^8) \approx 40$ bits, sufficient to render brute-force
guessing infeasible within the four-hour ticket lifetime.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\footnotesize
\caption{Printer-agnostic ticket generation}
\label{alg:ticket}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require confirmed queue number $q$, status-page base URL $U$, signing secret $K$
\Ensure persisted ticket payload and rendered ticket output
\State draw eight random characters from the unambiguous 32-character alphabet
\State format the short code as \texttt{XXXX-XXXX}
\State $jti \gets$ random 32-character hexadecimal nonce
\State $\mathrm{iat} \gets$ current timestamp; $\mathrm{exp} \gets \mathrm{iat}+4$ hours
\State create JWT header $h$ and payload $p=\{\mathrm{sub}:q,\mathrm{iat},\mathrm{exp},\mathrm{jti}\}$
\State sign $h.p$ with HMAC-SHA256 and secret $K$ to form token
\State create QR URL $U(q,\mathrm{token})$
\State persist queue number, short code, QR URL, token, expiry, and status
\If{prototype output is configured}
  \State render the payload as a PDF ticket
\Else
  \State send the same payload to the thermal-printer driver
\EndIf
\State \Return ticket payload
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Public Display Board with TTS Announcements}
\label{subsec:display-board}

A third presentation surface, alongside the staff dashboard and the
student mobile client, is a public display board mounted in the
service-office waiting area. The board renders the active queue and
announces newly assigned counter calls using the browser's built-in
Web Speech API. All speech synthesis runs client-side; no audio is
transmitted to any third-party service and no backend audio endpoint
is required, which is consistent with the privacy posture established
in Section~\ref{sec:ethics} (\S2.6).

A deduplication set indexed by the pair
$(\text{queue\_number}, \text{counter\_number})$ prevents the same
counter assignment from being announced more than once even when the
polling endpoint returns the same active queue across consecutive
intervals. Announcements are spoken sequentially with an $800\
\mathrm{ms}$ gap between utterances to avoid speech overlap when
several counter assignments are issued simultaneously.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\footnotesize
\caption{Public-display announcement deduplication}
\label{alg:display}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require latest counter assignments $A$, announced set $E$, speech queue $P$
\ForAll{$a \in A$}
  \State $k \gets (a.\mathrm{queue\_number}, a.\mathrm{counter\_number})$
  \If{$k \notin E$}
    \State add $k$ to $E$
    \State append announcement for $a$ to $P$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\While{$P$ is non-empty \textbf{and} speech engine is idle}
  \State speak the next announcement in $P$
  \State wait $800\ \mathrm{ms}$ before speaking the next item
\EndWhile
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
